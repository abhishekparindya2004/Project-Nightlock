About SPHEREx

"Exploring the universe through infrared light

Uncover the secrets of the universe with SPHEREx

SPHEREx is NASA’s space observatory designed to explore the universe in more than 100 wavelengths of infrared light. By mapping the entire sky, the mission helps scientists investigate the origins of galaxies, the history of cosmic evolution, and the distribution of essential ingredients for life.

Understanding the Origins of the Universe

"The universe began approximately 13.8 billion years ago in an event known as the Big Bang. Scientists believe that, during its earliest moments, the universe experienced an extremely rapid expansion called cosmic inflation. This expansion may have stretched tiny variations in the early universe across enormous distances, eventually helping to create the structures we observe today, including galaxies and clusters of galaxies. SPHEREx helps scientists investigate this history by studying the positions and distribution of millions of galaxies across the sky. Galaxies are not distributed completely randomly. Instead, they form large-scale patterns influenced by gravity and the conditions that existed in the early universe. By mapping these patterns in three dimensions, scientists can examine how matter is distributed throughout space and compare their observations with predictions made by different models of cosmic inflation. This can help researchers determine which explanations of the universe's earliest moments are most consistent with the evidence. SPHEREx uses infrared observations to estimate the distances to galaxies and study their properties. Its wide survey provides a large collection of measurements that scientists can analyse statistically. Importantly, SPHEREx does not directly observe cosmic inflation itself. Instead, it searches for clues preserved in the universe's large-scale structure. These clues help scientists understand how the universe evolved from its earliest conditions into the vast collection of galaxies we see today."

Mapping the Entire Sky

"One of SPHEREx's most important features is its ability to survey the entire sky rather than concentrating on just a small region. This allows scientists to build a broad picture of the universe and investigate objects and structures across enormous distances. SPHEREx observes the sky in more than 100 infrared spectral bands. A spectral band is a particular range of wavelengths of light. Different wavelengths reveal different information about astronomical objects, such as their temperatures, chemical compositions, and distances. Infrared light is especially useful because it can reveal information that ordinary visible-light observations may miss. For example, dust surrounding newly forming stars can block visible light, while infrared observations can help scientists study the material within and around these dusty regions. As SPHEREx scans the sky repeatedly, it collects measurements that can be combined into detailed maps. These maps help researchers identify galaxies, study the distribution of stars and cosmic dust, and investigate the chemical properties of interstellar clouds. Another major benefit is that SPHEREx can compare millions of objects using a consistent observing method. This is valuable for identifying large-scale patterns that might be difficult to recognise when studying only a small patch of space. The resulting dataset supports many different areas of astronomy, from studying the early universe to investigating how galaxies and planetary systems develop. In simple terms, SPHEREx acts like a cosmic surveyor, creating a detailed infrared map that scientists can use to explore the universe on both enormous and relatively small astronomical scales."

Searching for the Ingredients of Life

"Scientists studying the possibility of life beyond Earth need to understand where the basic ingredients of life exist in space and how these ingredients become part of planets. Two important examples are water and organic molecules. Water is essential for life as we know it, while organic molecules contain carbon and form the basis of many biological substances. These materials can exist in interstellar clouds, which are enormous regions of gas and dust where new stars and planetary systems may eventually form. SPHEREx uses infrared spectroscopy to investigate the chemical composition of these regions. Different molecules absorb or interact with particular wavelengths of infrared light, producing characteristic patterns that scientists can analyse. These patterns help researchers identify and estimate the abundance of substances such as water ice and certain carbon-containing molecules. Water ice is particularly important because it can collect on dust grains inside cold molecular clouds. When stars and planetary systems form from these clouds, some of this material may become incorporated into comets, asteroids, and planets. Studying where water ice exists can therefore help scientists understand how water may be delivered to developing planetary systems. SPHEREx also surveys many star-forming regions throughout the Milky Way, allowing researchers to compare their chemical compositions and environments. This can reveal how the availability of important molecules changes from one region to another. However, finding water ice or organic molecules does not mean that life exists there. Instead, SPHEREx helps scientists investigate whether the raw materials needed for life are widespread and how they become distributed during the formation of stars and planets."
